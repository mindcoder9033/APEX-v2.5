Based on the *Going Faster!* methodology, here's how the APEX PDF report should be structured to be **actionable** and **educational**—not just a data dump.

---

## PDF Report Structure

### Page 1: Executive Summary

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Header** | Track name, car, session date, number of laps analyzed |
| **Overall Grade** | A-F based on consistency and technique (e.g., "B+: Good line, needs braking work") |
| **Lap Time Delta** | "Your best lap was 1:23.85. Target was 1:23.12. You're **0.73s** off pace." |
| **Top 3 Issues** | Bullet list of the biggest problems (e.g., "1. Braking too early at T1", "2. Early apex at T9") |
| **Quick Wins** | "Fix these first for the biggest time gain" |

> *"The most important lesson is that in order to drive extraordinarily well, you have to use your head much more than your guts."*

---

### Page 2-3: Corner-by-Corner Breakdown

| Column | Content |
|--------|---------|
| **Corner #/Name** | e.g., "Turn 1 - Hairpin" |
| **Your Minimum Speed** | e.g., "52 mph" |
| **Target Minimum Speed** | e.g., "58 mph" |
| **Delta** | "-6 mph" (Highlight in RED) |
| **Your Brake Point** | e.g., "170m board" |
| **Target Brake Point** | e.g., "150m board" |
| **Grade** | A-F for that corner |
| **One-Line Fix** | e.g., "Brake 20m later and trail-brake to the apex" |

**Color Coding:** Green = Good / Red = Needs Work / Yellow = Marginal

---

### Page 4: Brake Analysis (The Brake Report)

**Graph:** Brake pressure trace overlay (Your lap vs. Target lap)

**Three Key Questions Answered:**

1. **Are you braking hard enough?**
   - "Peak brake pressure: 80%. Target: 95%. You're leaving 15% of stopping power unused."

2. **Are you trail-braking?**
   - "You release brakes instantly at turn-in. Try holding 30% brake pressure through the first 1/3 of the corner."

3. **Where do you lose time?**
   - "At T3, you brake 40ft earlier than target. This costs you 0.3s."

> *"It is amazing how many drivers, even at the Formula One level, think that the brakes are for slowing the car down."* — Mario Andretti

---

### Page 5: Throttle & Exit Speed Analysis

**Graph:** Throttle trace overlay (Your lap vs. Target lap)

**Key Insights:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| When do you get on throttle? | "At T5, you're 30m later than target. Get on power earlier." |
| Are you smooth or abrupt? | "You stab the throttle at T7. Squeeze it progressively over 1.5 seconds." |
| Exit speed comparison | "Your exit speed at T9: 92 mph. Target: 96 mph. You lose 0.2s down the straight." |

> *"Big chunks of lap time will come off by learning to maximize the exit speeds coming off the corners."*

---

### Page 6: Line Analysis

**Diagram:** Track map showing your line vs. target line (overlay)

**Diagnosis (from Chapter 3):**

| Symptom | Your Issue | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Steering *increases* after apex | Early apex at T2 | Turn in 1 car-length later |
| Road left at exit | Late apex at T8 | Turn in earlier |
| Can't unwind wheel on exit | Pinching T6 | Turn in later, apex later |

> *"If you can't unwind the steering wheel at corner exit, you're apexing too early."*

---

### Page 7: Consistency & Trend Analysis

**Graph:** All lap times in session (bar chart)

**Insights:**
- "Your lap times vary by 1.2s. Target variation: 0.3s. You're inconsistent."
- "Laps 1-5: slow. Laps 6-10: faster. Laps 11-15: falling off. Tire management?"
- "Your fastest lap was lap 8. Review what you did differently that lap."

---

### Page 8: Action Plan (The Most Important Page)

**Format:** Simple, numbered, actionable steps

| Priority | Action | Where to Practice | Expected Gain |
|----------|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Brake 20m later at T1 | Turn 1 | 0.3s |
| 2 | Turn in later at T9 | Carousel | 0.2s |
| 3 | Squeeze throttle at T7 | Turn 7 exit | 0.15s |
| 4 | Trail-brake into T3 | Turn 3 entry | 0.1s |
| **Total Possible Gain** | | | **0.75s** |

> *"Significant pieces of lap time come from being just a few mph slower than the fastest driver in a few significant places."*

---

### Page 9: Reference Points Quick Reference

**Table:** Your marks for next session

| Corner | Brake Point | Turn-In | Apex | Track-Out |
|--------|-------------|---------|------|-----------|
| T1 | 150m board | 2nd reflector | Red cone | End of curb |
| T2 | 100m board | Concrete patch | Center seam | Exit curb end |
| T3 | 200m board | Dark patch | Striped curb | Flat curb |

> *"The key is to establish a brake point for every corner and then work off of that point to suit the circumstance."*

## Summary: What Makes This a "Good" Report

| Checklist | ✓ |
|-----------|---|
| Tells me what I did *right* (builds confidence) | ✓ |
| Tells me what I did *wrong* (specific, not generic) | ✓ |
| Tells me *how to fix it* (actionable) | ✓ |
| Prioritizes fixes (so I know where to start) | ✓ |
| References the car, track, and my driving | ✓ |
| Uses clear language (no jargon overload) | ✓ |
| Includes visuals (graphs, track maps) | ✓ |
| One-page action plan for next session | ✓ |

---

## The Golden Rule

> *"The goal is not to make the driver feel bad. The goal is to make the driver **faster**. The report should feel like a coach, not a critic."*
